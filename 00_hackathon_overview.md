## 3. How it works (official flow)

1. Register individually or as a team.
2. Choose one of three challenges. You state a preference, but **Netcompany makes the final allocation** "to ensure a balanced and fair representation across all challenges". Rank your preferences deliberately.
3. Build your solution during the hackathon week.
4. Submit your project by the official deadline.
5. Pitch your idea to the committee.
6. Celebrate at the awards ceremony.

Each challenge "will include a clear problem statement and evaluation focus areas". These are **not published yet** and are expected at kick-off.

### Mentoring and support
- Mentors from Netcompany
- Technical and organisational assistance
- **Checkpoints** to help teams stay on track. Treat these as informal progress reviews and show something working at each one.
- The 2025 Thessaloniki kick-off agenda also covered "Communication channels, resources & tech support". Expect a Discord, Teams, or Slack-style channel to be announced at kick-off.

## 5. Rules (Terms & Conditions, summarised)

### Registration
- Must be completed online. The T&C on one page point to `https://netcompanyhackathon.gr/` and on the other to the netcompany.com About page. **As of 8 Oct 2026, netcompanyhackathon.gr shows a "site under construction" placeholder**, so use the "Register here" button on the netcompany.com page and keep a confirmation screenshot or email.
- False or incomplete information may lead to disqualification.

### Event rules
- All submissions must be **original and created during the hackathon period (12–27 Nov 2026)**.
- **Pre-existing codebases are permitted only with prior approval and must be disclosed at submission.** You *can* prepare learning and research beforehand, but don't write the project code in advance. If you want a starter template or boilerplate, ask for approval and disclose it.
- Participants must adhere to the event theme, if specified.
- **All work must be completed exclusively by registered Participants. External assistance is not permitted.** This means no friends, no paid help, and no unregistered teammates. Open-source libraries, public APIs, and AI coding assistants are not addressed. Assume libraries and APIs are fine, and **ask explicitly about AI coding tools** (see the draft questions in §7).

### Code of conduct
- Respectful behaviour. Harassment, discrimination, or disruption leads to immediate disqualification.
- Submissions must not contain viruses, trojans, worms, spyware, or other malicious code. **This matters for the Cybersecurity track:** keep any attack simulation clearly benign and sandboxed.

### Intellectual property
- **You keep the IP** in your submission.
- Netcompany gets a **non-exclusive, royalty-free licence** to use, modify, and showcase it *strictly for promotional purposes of the Hackathon*.
- You warrant that the submission doesn't infringe third-party IP. Check dataset and library licences.

### Judging
- **Criteria:** innovation · technical complexity · usability · ease of use · quality of delivery.
- Judges' decisions are final.

### Prizes
- €3,000 / €2,000 / €1,000. These are non-transferable and must be claimed **within 3 months**.
- Winners must provide full bank details of the account holder and a myAADE tax printout for each beneficiary.
- The organiser may modify or withdraw prizes in exceptional circumstances.

### Other
- **Liability:** You are responsible for your own equipment, data, and belongings.
- **Privacy:** Data is processed under GDPR. **By participating you consent to being photographed and filmed** for promotion.
- **Governing law:** Greek law, courts of Athens. The organiser may amend the T&C and will notify participants of significant changes.

- The press coverage and university announcements for the 2025 and 2026 Thessaloniki editions also don't state a format.

### What we can reasonably infer
| Phase | Likely format | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Kick-off (12 Nov) | **In-person** at Harokopio | A named venue. The 2025 Thessaloniki kick-off agenda starts with *"17:00–17:30 Arrivals // Registrations"*, followed by about 2h of speeches, challenge presentation, rules, and Q&A. That is a short evening event, not a working session. |
| Hackathon week (13–20 Nov) | **Self-organised / effectively remote** | No venue is listed for the build week, unlike every other milestone. A one-week sprint running alongside classes is almost certainly worked from home or university. Mentoring and checkpoints are likely online. |

| Pitching (26 Nov) | **In-person** at University of Piraeus | A named venue. "Teams present their solutions to the judging committee." |

### Constraints that apply regardless of location
- Every person contributing must be a **registered, eligible participant**. A remote teammate is fine **only if** they are an eligible student registered on the team. - There is no stated requirement that *all* team members attend the kick-off, pitch, or awards in person, and no stated penalty for absence. Whether a member can pitch via video link is **unknown**.

## 8. Signals from previous editions (Thessaloniki)

| Edition | Facts |
|---|---|
| **2025** (1st, 14–21 Feb) | 3 universities (UoM, AUTH, IHU). Teams of up to 5. Jury of **professors + Netcompany staff**. Winner: team "Journey Jolt" (UoM). |
| **2026** (2nd, 6–20 Mar) | **173 students, 67 teams.** Challenges: *GenAI as a System Designer*, *AI-Powered Knowledge Base System*, *AI-Powered Cybersecurity Control Center*. Jury: Netcompany executives only. |

**2026 winners, and what they tell us about what the judges reward:**
1. 🥇 **Solo participant** (IHU): a platform acting as a *digital advisor for businesses*. It turns operational data into a **live risk map** and suggests **ready-made response plans**. Takeaway: **decision support plus actionable output** wins, and team size isn't decisive.
2. 🥈 Team "Lurkas" (AUTH): real-time network monitoring, AI threat analysis, and **automatic response commands**. Takeaway: **closed loop** (detect → decide → act).
3. 🥉 Team "Kickback" (AUTH): an autonomous agent that turns an app description into cloud architecture, service selection, and infra diagrams. Takeaway: **agentic workflows with visual output**.

Common pattern: **an end-to-end working product with a visual, live dashboard that produces actionable recommendations**, not a model or notebook.

Expect about 60–80 teams in Athens (6 universities vs 3). That works out to **20+ teams per track** competing for 3 overall prizes.

## 9. Strategy notes

- **Pick the track by team strengths and judges.** Cyber has 2 specialist judges. Maritime has no domain judge, but it is the most "local" topic (pitching is literally in Piraeus). AI is the most popular and crowded.
- **Map the build to the 5 criteria explicitly**, and put them on a pitch slide:
  - *Innovation*: one sharp differentiator, not ten features.
  - *Technical complexity*: real architecture, not a thin LLM wrapper.
  - *Usability / ease of use*: a polished UI with a 30-second "aha" moment.
  - *Quality of delivery*: tests, CI, README, `docker compose up` one-liner, recorded backup demo.
- **Before 12 Nov** (allowed: learning, not project code): study the domain, prototype throwaway experiments, set up accounts and API keys, agree on stack and roles, and prepare a data-source shortlist.
- **Build week plan (8 days):** D1 architecture and skeleton deployed end-to-end · D2–5 core features · D6 the differentiator · D7 polish, tests, freeze · D8 docs, video, submit.
- **Pitch week:** rehearse; stress-test the demo offline; have a recorded fallback.
